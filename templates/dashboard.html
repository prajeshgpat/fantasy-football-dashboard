Offensive Plays Per Game — Is It Sustainable?

Regulation plays per game (overtime removed) this season vs last. A jump that comes from a faster neutral-script tempo, usually a new scheme, tends to stick. A jump from playing from behind, or from overtime, usually fades. Expected ROS is what to plan on for the rest of the season: it leans on this season more when tempo explains the change.

How the verdict is decidedNeutral tempo = seconds between snaps on the same drive while win probability is 20–80% (garbage time and clock-killing excluded); 1.5s faster or slower counts as a real change. Trailing 9+ = share of regulation snaps played down two scores or more; an 8-point rise explains extra volume as game script. Plays-per-drive and drives-per-game say whether the offense is sustaining drives or just getting more possessions. A change under 3 plays/G is Stable. Three games is a small sample, so treat early verdicts as leanings.

Defense vs. Position — Fantasy Points Allowed

PPR points allowed per game this season. Confidence (0–100) blends sample size, how far the team's 3-year baseline sits from league average, and how much of the damage came from top-12 players. WR1 / WR2 split each offense's receivers by their season targets for that team, so a defense that shuts down No. 1 receivers but leaks to No. 2s shows up.

How funnel calls workA defense that is stout at one spot pushes volume elsewhere. Pass funnel: tough vs RBs, soft vs WR/TE, so offenses throw more. Run funnel: tough vs WRs, soft vs RBs, so offenses run more. TE funnel: WRs blanketed, TEs soft, so targets go underneath. Strong means offenses actually changed behavior here (pass rate or TE target share vs their own norm in neutral game script); Lean means the points split exists but behavior hasn't moved yet. Calls are dropped when offenses do the opposite. Early in the season a single blowout can swing these, so check the G and Confidence columns.
